    Degree distribution

    How many nodes have each number of connections.

    What to look for

    A hump, no hubs. Most nodes have about the average number of connections, so the bars form a hump around it. Nobody becomes a hub.

    The phase transition. Set n = 200 and slide p from 0.001 up past 1/n ≈ 0.005. Below it the graph is dust (many components). Just above it, one giant component suddenly swallows most nodes.

    Almost no clustering (about p): two neighbours of a node are rarely linked to each other.

    Hubs and a long tail. A few nodes collect a huge number of links while most have only m. The bars have a long tail to the right.

    Turn on Log–log. The points fall roughly on a straight line: the signature of a power law, the "scale-free" property of the web, citation networks and software dependencies.

    Compare with Erdős–Rényi at the same average degree (m = 2 ≈ p = 4/n): here the biggest hub has several times more links.

    Use the Circle layout. The band around the edge is the regular ring of neighbours; every chord across the middle is a rewired shortcut.

    The small-world effect. At p = 0 clustering is high but paths are long. Slide p to about 0.05: the average path length drops sharply while clustering barely moves. High clustering plus short paths is a small world, like "six degrees of separation".

    At p = 1 the graph is almost random and clustering collapses.
